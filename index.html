Meet the team

We're a group of young engineers at Harlingen Collegiate High, working to solve a problem for future astronauts. Our tool cuts aluminum on Mars using only compressed air, and every part is built to be swapped out and used with thick gloves. We design it, test it, and keep improving it.

Our prototype

Prototype progress

Our current focus is proving the actuator can extend and retract with compressed air, then building toward a complete pneumatic hacksaw.

What we built

Pneumatic actuator prototype

Team Air-osmith has built a pneumatic actuator as the base component for an eventual pneumatic hacksaw. We designed the prototype around a working pressure target of up to 135 psi because we were concerned about how well it would hold up after repeated use.

The cylinder barrel uses braided PVC pipe because it resists physical wear, is transparent, and is easy to find. We modeled the end caps in 3D and printed them using Bambu PLA Tough+ filament to help limit air leakage. Printed components were made with six layers to reduce porous gaps where air could escape. O-rings, epoxy, and other sealing materials were also used to help close gaps between parts.

What it does

How the actuator works

The actuator needs to extend and retract so it can move a future hacksaw blade back and forth through an aluminum angle. We chose a 5/2-way, single-solenoid valve to control the double-acting cylinder. A double-acting actuator uses compressed air to drive both directions, so the valve switches the air supply and exhaust between the two strokes.

We selected 8 mm PCU tubing for its stated pressure rating of about 150 psi, low weight, flexibility, and durability. Elbow fittings connect the tubing to the end caps and help route air from the compressor into the system.

Main components

  • Braided PVC cylinder barrel
  • Three modeled end caps and a blocker
  • Wooden actuator rod and supports
  • 8 mm PCU tubing and elbow fittings
  • 5/2-way single-solenoid valve
  • Medium-sized air compressor

Component list reflects the team's reported prototype status as of October 23, 2026.

What we need to continue working on

Next steps in the workshop

Budget limits and late project changes from the NASA HUNCH team made it difficult to gather every resource for our original design. We focused first on getting the main action working: extending and retracting the actuator using pneumatics alone.

Our next step is to replace the temporary wooden actuator rod with a more suitable, durable option. The main goal is to attach a base frame so we can mount the hacksaw frame and blade. We also want to improve the way the parts are assembled and finished so the prototype is cleaner and easier to work with.
